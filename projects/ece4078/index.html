ECE4078 · July–October 2025

Autonomous Robot Navigation, SLAM & Fruit Detection

A camera-equipped mobile robot designed to map an unfamiliar arena, detect target fruits and autonomously navigate towards them while avoiding obstacles.

PythonSLAM / EKFArUco MarkersObject DetectionDijkstra / A*Team of 6

The challenge

Starting from the centre of an arena, the robot needed to localise itself with reference to ArUco markers, build a map, identify objects from a fruit shopping list, and reach each confirmed target within 10 cm. The robot also had to avoid known obstacles, search for hidden targets and evaluate map accuracy against a physically measured reference layout.

Autonomous decision-making workflow

01 · Localise & mapObserve ArUco markers; maintain position estimates using SLAM and an Extended Kalman Filter.
02 · Detect candidatesIdentify possible fruits from camera input and record their estimated locations in the map.
03 · Plan & verifyPrioritise nearby candidates, navigate around obstacles, and confirm the target at closer range.
04 · Search & repeatSignal when within 10 cm; plan the next route or scan near arena corners for hidden targets.

My contribution

As part of a six-member team, I worked particularly closely with one teammate to plan and integrate the end-to-end autonomous workflow. My contribution centred on:

  • Designing the high-level sequence for mapping, candidate fruit detection, target prioritisation, navigation and simulated collection.
  • Integrating perception, EKF/SLAM localisation, path planning and robot movement into a coordinated search workflow.
  • Repeatedly calibrating the robot and adjusting localisation/scale to minimise drift between the estimated and real-world map.
  • Testing robot behaviour and investigating errors observed during longer autonomous runs.

System design

The team's object-recognition model was trained for capsicum, orange, lemon, lime, pumpkin, potato, garlic, tomato and pear. Detected objects were first added as suspected locations, then approached for closer confirmation.

ArUco marker observations provided reference points for localisation and re-localisation, while Dijkstra/A* path-planning methods supported navigation between potential targets. If no visible candidates remained, the robot visited further observation positions near the corners and rotated to scan for hidden objects.

Results and engineering lessons

Functional autonomous search and navigation demonstrated

The physical robot performed the intended mapping, detection, target-approach and search behaviours. One concealed target required extra exploration before it was found. During extended operation, accumulated position-estimation error caused the robot to overestimate some distances and occasionally contact obstacles. This highlighted the importance of calibration, repeated re-localisation and uncertainty management in autonomous robotics. No numerical mapping-precision result is claimed here.
